XoxDairyQueen

SOURCE MIRROR // official wiki ->

xoxDairyQueen, known as Lucille Collins in real life, was the leader of Blacklisted Clan.

Appearance

xoxDairyQueen's Minecraft skin appears to fairly resemble her real life appearance, featuring blonde hair, a green and brown tunic, and black pants. She also wore glasses.

Personality

xoxDairyQueen is a person who likes reading, writing, Minecraft, art and delving into unnecessary rabbit holes. She wishes to be a writer but can't because she keeps "delving into the heinous crevices of this game", an addiction that will never be satiated. She is also an artist, and she is close to the members of Blacklisted. She can get irritated, or even pissed off pretty quickly. Lucy is autistic. It's possible that during her time on the server, she was so passionate about the clans that she unknowingly was cruel to her friends. She often tries to rationalize things. Even when she is mad she can care. She wishes that she never played on the server along with the rest of Blacklisted. She has enough courage and dedication to interview a void entity for more than days. She cares a lot about Neb and Carlos but she only tolerates Dom because the other two like him.

It has never sat right with me, this insistence that all loneliness must be a form of suffering. You could wander the eternal halls of purgatory, passing door after door without ever finding another soul behind them, and still remain content within your own skin. Your footsteps might be the only sound left in that endless place, but at least they would belong to you. At the end of it all, what truly matters is autonomy. The quiet certainty that your thoughts are your own, that your hands move because you command them to, that no one else has the right to decide what shape your life must take. This is why it vexes me when others claim we must belong to another when that is certainly not the case. We may offer pieces of ourselves. Our time, our trust, the softer parts we usually keep hidden; but no person can ever be promised to another. People leave. Sometimes through choice, sometimes through cruelty, and sometimes with such terrible suddenness that the warmth has not yet faded from their chair before the world begins referring to them in the past tense. One moment, they are speaking. The next, their mouth is still, their eyes fixed upon something no one else can see and every word you thought you still had time to say becomes useless inside you. That is the fragility of surrounding yourself with others. You mistake their presence as permanence. A coat left hanging by the door does not mean its owner will return, you know? I only think people should become more comfortable with absence. With empty chairs, cold bedsides and the possibility that those they love may be taken without warning.
